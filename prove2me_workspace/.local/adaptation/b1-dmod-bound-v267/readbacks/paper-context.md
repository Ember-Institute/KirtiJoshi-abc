Proposition 1.8(ii), page 19, identifies the field of definition used for the moduli invariant.

> The finite extension $k_E$ of $k$ determined by $G_E$ is the minimal field of definition of $E_{\overline{k}}$, i.e., the field generated over $k$ by the $j$-invariant of $E_{\overline{k}}$.

Corollary 2.2(ii), page 42, assumes a lift $x_E\in U_X(F)\cap U_X(\overline{\mathbb Q})^{\leq d}$ in the specified subset and outside the exceptional set. It then names the intermediate fields.

> Write $F_{\mathrm{mod}}$ for the minimal field of definition of the corresponding point $\in\mathcal M_{\mathrm{ell}}(\overline{\mathbb Q})$ and
>
> $$F_{\mathrm{mod}}\subseteq F_{\mathrm{tpd}}\stackrel{\mathrm{def}}{=}F_{\mathrm{mod}}(E_{F_{\mathrm{mod}}}[2])\subseteq F$$
>
> for the “tripodal” intermediate field obtained from $F_{\mathrm{mod}}$ by adjoining the fields of definition of the 2-torsion points of any model of $E_F\times_F\overline{\mathbb Q}$ over $F_{\mathrm{mod}}$ [cf. Proposition 1.8, (ii), (iii)].

The proof of Corollary 2.2(ii), page 47, uses the estimates $20\cdot d_{\mathrm{mod}}\leq d_{\mathrm{mod}}^*\leq\delta$. In the adaptation, `fieldOfModuli` is the subfield generated by $j$ inside the selected curve field. The Legendre formula puts $j$ in the subfield generated by the coordinate of $x$. The local degree bound follows from that inclusion and the degree of the chosen point. It supplies an input to the numerical estimate in the reference code.
