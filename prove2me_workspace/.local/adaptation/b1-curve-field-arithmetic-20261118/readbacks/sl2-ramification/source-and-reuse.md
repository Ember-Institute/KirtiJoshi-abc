For the square-root descent, put $d=-c_4c_6$ for the multiplicative model. A square root in $K$ maps to the completion. Otherwise, the source adjoins $\sqrt d$ and chooses a place above the given place. If conjugation fixes that place, it acts on the completion and fixes the full $\ell$-torsion by the torsion count. The Tate sign theorem then gives a conjugation-fixed square root of $d$, although conjugation exchanges the two roots. If conjugation moves the place, the relative residue degree is one. The source lifts the residue of $\sqrt d$ and applies Hensel's lemma in the original completion.
