# Extension of constants for function-field normalization

## English

Let $k\to K\to\Omega$ be a tower of fields and let $t\in\Omega$. Write $k[t]$ and $k(t)$ for the subalgebra and intermediate field generated by $t$ over $k$ in $\Omega$. Use the same notation over $K$. The inclusions $k[t]\hookrightarrow K[t]$ and $k(t)\hookrightarrow K(t)$ preserve the image of each element in $\Omega$. An element of $\Omega$ integral over $k[t]$ is integral over $K[t]$, since mapping the coefficients of a monic equation preserves the equation. [Base rings and fields](../../../../../ArithmeticTeichmullerSpaces/ArithmeticTeichmullerSpaces/Act1Scene1/FunctionFieldNormalization.lean#L50) · [Inclusions and integrality](../../../../Solutions/Source_ReferenceIutCanLiftConstantExtension.lean#L30)

For intermediate fields $F/k(t)$ and $F'/K(t)$ inside $\Omega$, suppose $F\subseteq F'$. The ring `coordRing t F` is the integral closure of $k[t]$ in $F$. The map `constantExtensionMap` from `coordRing t F` to `coordRing t F'` is injective and sends every element to the same element of $\Omega$. For inclusions $F\subseteq L$ and $F'\subseteq L'$ over their respective base fields, with $L\subseteq L'$ in $\Omega$, the maps for extension of constants commute with the normalization-ring inclusions `ringMap`. [Normalization rings and compatibility](../../../../Solutions/Source_ReferenceIutCanLiftConstantExtension.lean#L67) · [Pi1 definition](https://github.com/lana-agents/pi1/blob/ca7def953193f861a462aa0831eec8f0a31ff69f/Pi1/Orbicurve/BaseChange.lean#L89)

Assume $t$ is transcendental over $K$. Then $K[t]\cap k(t)=k[t]$ inside $\Omega$. For an element in the intersection, equate its polynomial expression over $K$ to a rational expression over $k$ with a nonzero denominator. Transcendence gives a polynomial identity after clearing the denominator. Dividing the numerator by the denominator over $k$ gives a remainder of smaller degree. The identity over $K$ forces the remainder to vanish. [Polynomial intersection](../../../../Solutions/Source_ReferenceIutCanLiftConstantExtension.lean#L95) · [Pi1 proof](https://github.com/lana-agents/pi1/blob/ca7def953193f861a462aa0831eec8f0a31ff69f/Pi1/Orbicurve/Descent.lean#L27)

Suppose also that $k$ is algebraically closed. Then $k(t)$ is relatively algebraically closed in $K(t)$. To prove this, multiply an element of $K(t)$ algebraic over $k(t)$ by a nonzero element of $k[t]$ to make it integral over $k[t]$. Since $K[t]$ is integrally closed in $K(t)$, the product lies in $K[t]$. Its polynomial coefficients are integral over $k$ and thus belong to $k$. Dividing by the multiplier puts the original element in $k(t)$. [Relative algebraic closedness](../../../../Solutions/Source_ReferenceIutCanLiftConstantExtension.lean#L190) · [Pi1 proof](https://github.com/lana-agents/pi1/blob/ca7def953193f861a462aa0831eec8f0a31ff69f/Pi1/Orbicurve/BaseChange.lean#L640)

For any $x\in\Omega$ algebraic over $k(t)$, mapping the coefficients of its minimal polynomial from $k(t)$ to $K(t)$ gives its minimal polynomial over $K(t)$. The latter divides the image of the former, so its coefficients are integral over $k(t)$. Relative algebraic closedness puts these coefficients in $k(t)$. The monic polynomial with these coefficients still vanishes at $x$, so minimality gives the equality after mapping. If $x$ is also integral over $K[t]$, its minimal polynomial over $K(t)$ has coefficients in $K[t]$. The equality puts the coefficients of the original polynomial in $K[t]\cap k(t)=k[t]$, so $x$ is integral over $k[t]$.
