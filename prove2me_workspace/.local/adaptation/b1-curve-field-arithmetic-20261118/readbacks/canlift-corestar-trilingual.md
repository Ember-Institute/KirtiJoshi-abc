## English

Let $E$ be an elliptic Weierstrass curve over a characteristic-zero field $k$. Let $U$ and $S$ be its unsigned and signed normalization presentations at level $1$ with trivial subgroup. Let $\pi:U\to S$ be the canonical sign quotient cover. The predicate `NormalizationCoreStar E` says that each cover $Z\to U$ admits at most one cover $Z\to S$. [Adapted predicate](../../../../Solutions/Source_ReferenceIutCanLiftCoreStar.lean#L33) · [Quotient cover](../../../../Solutions/Source_ReferenceIutCanLiftPresentation.lean#L82) · [Pi1 CoreStar](https://github.com/lana-agents/pi1/blob/ca7def953193f861a462aa0831eec8f0a31ff69f/Pi1/Orbicurve/Lefschetz.lean#L37)

The declaration `normalizationCoreStar_of_isCoreOf` uses the cover $Z\to U$ and the identity of $Z$ to exhibit a common cover. The core property gives uniqueness of $Z\to S$. In the reverse direction, `normalizationIsCore_of_normalizationCoreStar` applies `isCoreOf_of_unique_covers` to $\pi$ and the multiplicity-one condition on $U$. [Core to uniqueness](../../../../Solutions/Source_ReferenceIutCanLiftCoreStar.lean#L36) · [Uniqueness to core](../../../../Solutions/Source_ReferenceIutCanLiftCoreStar.lean#L41) · [Core criterion](../../../../Solutions/Source_ReferenceIutCanLiftCoreCriterion.lean#L74) · [Pi1 core equivalence](https://github.com/lana-agents/pi1/blob/ca7def953193f861a462aa0831eec8f0a31ff69f/Pi1/Orbicurve/Lefschetz.lean#L52)

Choose an embedding $\iota:k(E)\to\Omega$ into an algebraically closed field and set $t=\iota(x_E)$ and $y=\iota(y_E)$. The quadratic model identifies the rings of $U$ and $S$ with the integral closures of $k[t]$ in $k(t)(y)$ and $k(t)$, respectively. Under these identifications, the ring map of $\pi$ is the inclusion. The proof compares ramification indices at an ideal lying over each closed point of the $t$-line. This identifies the transported multiplicity of $S$ with `signQuotientMultiplicity t y`. [Commuting inclusion](../../../../Solutions/Source_ReferenceIutCanLiftQuadraticModel.lean#L226) · [Multiplicity comparison](../../../../Solutions/Source_ReferenceIutCanLiftCoreStar.lean#L130) · [Pi1 quadratic coordinate criterion](https://github.com/lana-agents/pi1/blob/ca7def953193f861a462aa0831eec8f0a31ff69f/Pi1/Orbicurve/Lefschetz.lean#L404)

For `quadraticCoverRigidity_of_normalizationCoreStar`, take a finite field $F$ over $k(t)$ containing $k(t)(y)$, with normalization unramified over that of $k(t)(y)$. Its normalization with multiplicity $1$ gives a cover $\varphi:Z\to U$. An injective integral map from the $t$-line with the prescribed sign ramification gives a cover $\psi:Z\to S$. Uniqueness gives $\psi=\pi\circ\varphi$. The commuting inclusion identifies the original map with the canonical inclusion. [Adapted forward implication](../../../../Solutions/Source_ReferenceIutCanLiftCoreStar.lean#L204) · [Pi1 forward implication](https://github.com/lana-agents/pi1/blob/ca7def953193f861a462aa0831eec8f0a31ff69f/Pi1/Orbicurve/Lefschetz.lean#L74)

For `normalizationCoreStar_of_quadraticCoverRigidity`, start with a cover $\varphi:Z\to U$. The normalization embedding theorem realizes its ring map in $\Omega$. At every maximal ideal the cover identity gives $e\cdot m_Z=1$, so $m_Z=1$ and the realized field is unramified over $k(t)(y)$. Transport any cover $\psi:Z\to S$ through the ring equivalences. Quadratic rigidity makes its ring map canonical, so $\psi=\pi\circ\varphi$. The theorem `normalizationCoreStar_iff_quadraticCoverRigidity` pairs these two implications. [Normalization embedding](../../../../Solutions/Source_ReferenceIutCanLiftNormalizationEmbed.lean#L67) · [Adapted reverse implication](../../../../Solutions/Source_ReferenceIutCanLiftCoreStar.lean#L267) · [Adapted equivalence](../../../../Solutions/Source_ReferenceIutCanLiftCoreStar.lean#L296) · [Pi1 ambient-field equivalence](https://github.com/lana-agents/pi1/blob/ca7def953193f861a462aa0831eec8f0a31ff69f/Pi1/Orbicurve/Lefschetz.lean#L122)

Over $\mathbb C$, `CanLift27Complex` asserts `IsCoreOf U S` for curves with nonexceptional $j$-invariant. The two equivalences express that conclusion as cover uniqueness and as rigidity at the chosen quadratic coordinates. The countable-model proof uses both directions to pass between the geometric and ring-map statements for the same coordinates.
