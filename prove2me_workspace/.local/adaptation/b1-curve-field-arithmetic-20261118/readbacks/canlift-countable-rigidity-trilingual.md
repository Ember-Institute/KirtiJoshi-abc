## English

Let $K$ be an algebraically closed field of characteristic zero, $\Omega$ an algebraically closed extension, and $E/K$ an elliptic Weierstrass curve. Let $t$ be transcendental over $K$, with $y$ satisfying the Weierstrass coordinate equation at $t$. The theorem `quadraticCoverRigidity_of_countable_models` proves `QuadraticCoverRigidity K t y` from the same rigidity over every countable algebraically closed subfield $k\subseteq K$ and every elliptic model $W/k$ that maps to $E$. The lower rigidity hypothesis uses the same $t,y$ in $\Omega$. [Adapted rigidity theorem](../../../../Solutions/Source_ReferenceIutCanLiftCountableRigidity.lean#L27) · [Pi1 countable-model proof](https://github.com/lana-agents/pi1/blob/ca7def953193f861a462aa0831eec8f0a31ff69f/Pi1/Orbicurve/LefschetzMain.lean#L114)

A test of rigidity supplies a finite $L/K(t)$ that contains $y$ and an admissible map $g:K[t]\to A_L$, where $A_L$ is the integral closure of $K[t]$ in $L$. Set $s=g(t)$. The theorem `exists_countable_cover_field` chooses one model $W/k$ for this test. It uses the coefficients of a primitive element's minimal polynomial, polynomial expressions for $s,y$, and the Weierstrass equation. It gives a finite $F/k(t)$ inside $L$ with $s,y\in F$ and $L=K(t)(F)$. [Choice of small field](../../../../Solutions/Source_ReferenceIutCanLiftCountableCoverField.lean#L93) · [Pi1 primitive element step](https://github.com/lana-agents/pi1/blob/ca7def953193f861a462aa0831eec8f0a31ff69f/Pi1/Orbicurve/LefschetzMain.lean#L129)

The model equality puts the coefficients of the quadratic equation in $k(t)$. The theorem `quadraticCoverMap_eq_of_descended` uses $s,y\in F$ to descend the unramified condition and the sign-quotient ramification identity. It applies the lower-field rigidity hypothesis to the map with parameter $s$. Evaluating the resulting equality at the generator gives $s=t$, so the original map $g$ is the canonical inclusion. [Application of lower-field rigidity](../../../../Solutions/Source_ReferenceIutCanLiftCountableRigidity.lean#L43) · [Map descent](../../../../Solutions/Source_ReferenceIutCanLiftDescendedCover.lean#L135) · [Pi1 comparison](https://github.com/lana-agents/pi1/blob/ca7def953193f861a462aa0831eec8f0a31ff69f/Pi1/Orbicurve/LefschetzMain.lean#L242)

The theorem `normalizationCoreStar_of_countable_models` uses the function-field coordinates of $E$ in its algebraic closure. For each lower model $W$, it builds an embedding of $k(W)$ that sends its two coordinates to those same $t,y$. Lower `NormalizationCoreStar` gives rigidity there. The first theorem gives rigidity over $K$, and the reverse `CoreStar` implication gives `NormalizationCoreStar E`. [Adapted countable CoreStar theorem](../../../../Solutions/Source_ReferenceIutCanLiftCountableRigidity.lean#L54) · [Embedding at prescribed coordinates](../../../../Solutions/Source_ReferenceIutCanLiftQuadraticModel.lean#L71) · [Pi1 CoreStar theorem](https://github.com/lana-agents/pi1/blob/ca7def953193f861a462aa0831eec8f0a31ff69f/Pi1/Orbicurve/LefschetzMain.lean#L114)

The theorem `normalizationIsCore_of_countable_models` uses the core property of the signed normalization on each countable model to obtain its `NormalizationCoreStar` condition. The previous theorem gives that condition over $K$. The core criterion then proves that the actual signed normalization is the core of the unsigned normalization of $E$. [Adapted core theorem](../../../../Solutions/Source_ReferenceIutCanLiftCountableRigidity.lean#L80) · [Pi1 core equivalence](https://github.com/lana-agents/pi1/blob/ca7def953193f861a462aa0831eec8f0a31ff69f/Pi1/Orbicurve/Lefschetz.lean#L52)

At universe $0$, the last theorem provides the implication required by `hsubfield` in `canLift27_of_complex_of_descent`. That assembly takes `CanLift27Complex` and descent along field embeddings as further inputs. It embeds each countable model in $\mathbb C$, applies the complex theorem at the nonexceptional curve, and descends the result to the model. The countable-model step passes to the algebraic closure of the original field. The embedding descent then returns to the original field.
