## English

- `Presentation` uses the integral closure of `k[x_G]` in the fixed field `Ω(E)^{Hgp(E,ℓ,M,pm)}`, viewed over `k(x_G)`. The base field `k` has characteristic zero, and `E` is elliptic. The sign flag lets `Hgp` use the negative of the selected division point, in addition to the positive sign and the allowed translations. The normalization ring is a finite-type Dedekind domain over `k` with the field `not_isField`. The multiplicities are positive at maximal ideals, with finite support for the values other than `1`. The unsigned presentation has multiplicity `1` everywhere. The signed multiplicity is `ramificationIdxIn` for the inclusion into the unsigned normalization. At any prime of the unsigned ring above a given signed prime, the local index equals that multiplicity. This gives the ramification law for `signedNormalizationCover`, whose coordinate map is the injective integral inclusion from the signed ring to the unsigned ring. - At `ℓ = 1` and `M = ⊥`, the unsigned ring is isomorphic to the affine coordinate ring of `E`, and the signed ring to `k[X]`. The companion branch lemma gives multiplicity `2` at a maximal ideal exactly when it contains the image of the two-torsion polynomial, and `1` otherwise. `CanLift27` asks that this signed presentation be the core of the unsigned one when `j(E)` avoids `{0, 1728, 488095744/125, 1556068/81}`. In this category, `IsCoreOf X C` requires a common cover of `X` and `C`, and exactly one cover `Y → C` for each `Y` with a common cover with `X`. - `PolynomialDegree` takes a nonconstant `p ∈ K[X]` over an algebraically closed field of characteristic zero. For a three-element set `E₂`, let `m` be `2` on `E₂` and `1` elsewhere. The hypothesis is `e_p(w)m(w) = m(p(w))` at every `w ∈ K`, with the same `m` on both sides and `e_p(w)` the root multiplicity of `p(X) − p(w)` at `w`. For `D = deg p` and `S = p⁻¹(E₂)`, fiber counts give `3D = 3 + 2|S ∖ E₂|`. The roots of `p′` are exactly `S ∖ E₂` and are simple, so `|S ∖ E₂| = D − 1`. These equations give `D = 1`. - `CoverDescent` takes covers `ψ : Z → Y` and `χ : Z → C`, a `k`-algebra map `g : C.A → Y.A`, and the given factorization `ψ.f.comp g = χ.f`. Injectivity of `χ.f` implies injectivity of `g`. Integrality of `χ.f` descends to `g` along the injective `ψ.f`. Integrality of `ψ.f` and its zero kernel give a maximal ideal `q` of `Z.A` above each maximal `w` of `Y.A`. The Dedekind condition on `Y.A`, the domain condition on `Z.A`, and injectivity of `ψ.f` give `e(q/C.A) = e(w/C.A)e(q/Y.A)`. The two input cover laws then yield `e(w/C.A)Y.mult(w) = C.mult(w.comap g)`. The rings already carry their Dedekind structures, which include integral closedness. The theorem infers integrality of the map `g`. - For the complex core, `CanLift27ComplexXDescends` supplies the missing factorization by descending the image of the `x`-coordinate through each common cover. Uniqueness requires `CanLift27ComplexUniqueCover` or, with that coordinate descent, `CanLift27ComplexHemiRigid`. The latter needs the polynomial degree criterion, the identification of local indices and branch multiplicities on the signed normalization, and the affine rigidity result that forces `p = X` when `j ≠ 0,1728`. To obtain `CanLift27.{0}` from the complex case, the Lefschetz assembly takes two additional inputs. It takes descent of the core along field embeddings and detection of the core over algebraically closed fields by countable algebraically closed subfields.
