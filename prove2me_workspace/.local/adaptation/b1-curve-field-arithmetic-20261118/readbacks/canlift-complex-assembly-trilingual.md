# CanLift 2.7 over the complex numbers

## English

### Uniformization and commensurator hypothesis

The input `hU` to `canLift27Complex_of_uniformization` ranges over every Weierstrass curve $E$ over $\mathbb C$ with an `E.IsElliptic` instance. The hypothesis applies when $j(E)$ avoids these rational values from `canLiftExceptionalJ`.

$$
J_{\mathrm{exc}}=\left\{0,\ 1728,\ \frac{488095744}{125},\ \frac{1556068}{81}\right\}.
$$

For each such $E$, there exist $G_1,G_2\in\mathrm{SL}_2(\mathbb R)$ and $\pi\colon\mathbb C\to\mathbb C\times\mathbb C$. Write $\Gamma=\langle G_1,G_2\rangle$ for the subgroup generated by the two matrices. The condition `IsUniformization E G₁ G₂ π` requires two properties at every point of the upper half-plane $\mathbb H$. The map $\pi$ is complex differentiable and its vector derivative is nonzero. Its image from $\mathbb H$ is exactly the set of solutions of the affine Weierstrass equation. Two points of $\mathbb H$ have the same image if and only if they are in the same $\Gamma$-orbit. For these same data, `hU` also requires

$$
\forall g\in\operatorname{Comm}_{\mathrm{SL}_2(\mathbb R)}(\Gamma),\
\forall \tau\in\mathbb H,\qquad
\bigl(\pi(g\cdot\tau)\bigr)_1=\bigl(\pi(\tau)\bigr)_1.
$$

Here $g\cdot\tau$ is the Möbius action. The commensurator consists of the elements $g$ for which $\Gamma\cap g\Gamma g^{-1}$ has finite index in both subgroups. ### Fixing the normalization coordinate

The theorem `IsUniformization.fixes_normalization_x` applies to any elliptic Weierstrass curve over $\mathbb C$ with a given uniformization and the commensurator condition above. Let $A$ be the coordinate ring of `normalizationPresentation E 1 ⊥ false`, the level-one unsigned normalization for the trivial subgroup. Write $x_E$ for `normalizationBaseX E`, the element corresponding to the affine Weierstrass $x$-coordinate. [Fixed-coordinate theorem](../../../../Solutions/Source_ReferenceIutCanLiftFixX.lean#L44) · [Affine coordinate identification](../../../../Solutions/Source_ReferenceIutCanLiftAnalyticInput.lean#L20)

Take any commutative ring $B$ in `Type` with $\mathbb C$- and $A$-algebra structures that form a scalar tower $\mathbb C\to A\to B$. Assume $B$ is a Dedekind domain and a finite, faithful $A$-module. For every maximal ideal $\mathfrak m$ of $B$, assume the relative ramification index over $A$ is $1$. Write $i\colon A\to B$ for the algebra map. The theorem gives

$$
\forall \sigma\in\operatorname{Aut}_{\mathbb C\text{-alg}}(B),\qquad
\sigma(i(x_E))=i(x_E).
$$

The proof uses the same injective evaluation map $\iota_0\colon A\to(\mathbb H\to\mathbb C)$ for the local presentations and the holomorphic lift $\iota$. For every $\tau$, $\iota_0(x_E)(\tau)=(\pi(\tau))_1$. The invariant-coordinate lemma represents holomorphic $\Gamma$-invariant functions of polynomial growth in that coordinate by elements of the affine coordinate ring. The Möbius lift realizes $\sigma$ as $\iota(\tau)(\sigma b)=\iota(g\cdot\tau)(b)$ for a $g$ in the commensurator and all $\tau,b$. Applying the commensurator condition to $i(x_E)$ gives equality at every $\tau$. The lift vanishing lemma gives equality in $B$. ### The complex core and descent in characteristic zero

Under `hU`, the assembly proves `CanLift27Complex`. For every complex elliptic Weierstrass curve with $j(E)\notin J_{\mathrm{exc}}$, let $X_E$ and $C_E$ be the level-one presentations with trivial subgroup and sign flags `false` and `true`, respectively. The presentation $X_E$ models $E$ with its origin removed. The signed presentation $C_E$ models the quotient by $[-1]$. The conclusion is `IsCoreOf X_E C_E`. The signed quotient map $X_E\to C_E$ and the identity on $X_E$ give the required common cover. For each affine orbicurve presentation $Y$ over $\mathbb C$ with a common cover with $X_E$, there is exactly one cover $Y\to C_E$. [Complex core statement](../../../../Solutions/Source_ReferenceIutCanLiftPresentation.lean#L113) · [Definition of core](../../../../Solutions/Source_ReferenceIutCanLiftPresentation.lean#L25)

The fixed-coordinate theorem applies to the coordinate ring of each cover of $X_E$. Take a common cover with maps $\phi\colon Z\to X_E$ and $\psi\colon Z\to Y$. The Galois closure argument gives an element $y\in Y.A$ with $\psi.f(y)=\phi.f(x_E)$ in $Z.A$. Identify the signed coordinate ring with $\mathbb C[X]$. Evaluation at $y$ defines the ring map to $Y.A$ for the cover $Y\to C_E$. The rigidity theorem makes every self-cover of $C_E$ the identity when $j(E)\ne 0,1728$. This gives uniqueness in the core statement. 1. For any embedding $f\colon k\hookrightarrow K$ of characteristic-zero fields and any elliptic Weierstrass curve $E/k$, the core property descends from $E$ with coefficients mapped along $f$ to $E$.
2. Let $K$ be algebraically closed of characteristic zero and $E/K$ an elliptic Weierstrass curve. For each countable algebraically closed subfield $L\subseteq K$, consider all elliptic Weierstrass curves $W/L$ whose coefficient map to $K$ gives $E$. If the core property holds for each such $W$, it holds for $E$.

The proof passes to an algebraic closure of the original field. It embeds each such $L$ into $\mathbb C$ and uses preservation of the exceptional-value condition.
