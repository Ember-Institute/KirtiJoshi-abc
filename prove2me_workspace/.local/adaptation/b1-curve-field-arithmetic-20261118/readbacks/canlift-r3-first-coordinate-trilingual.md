# First-coordinate rigidity and the complex CanLift theorem

## `fst_smul_eq_of_invariant_polynomial`

### English

Let $W$ be an elliptic Weierstrass curve over $\mathbb C$ with $j(W)\notin\{0,1728\}$. Let $A,B\in\mathrm{SL}_2(\mathbb R)$, let $\Gamma=\langle A,B\rangle$ be the subgroup they generate, and let $\pi=(x,y)\colon\mathbb C\to\mathbb C^2$. On the upper half-plane $\mathbb H=\{z\in\mathbb C:\operatorname{Im}z>0\}$, assume that $\pi$ is complex differentiable at every $z$ with $\pi'(z)\ne(0,0)$, and that its image is exactly the set of complex solutions $(X,Y)$ of the affine equation $Y^2+a_1XY+a_3Y=X^3+a_2X^2+a_4X+a_6$ for the coefficients $a_i$ of $W$. For every $z,w\in\mathbb H$, assume that $\pi(z)=\pi(w)$ if and only if $\gamma\cdot z=w$ for some $\gamma\in\Gamma$, where $\cdot$ denotes the Möbius action of determinant-one real matrices. Independently take any subgroup $N\le\mathrm{SL}_2(\mathbb R)$ and any polynomial $s\in\mathbb C[X]$ of degree at least one. Assume $s(x(n\cdot z))=s(x(z))$ for every $n\in N$ and $z\in\mathbb H$, and assume that for every $z,w\in\mathbb H$ with $s(x(z))=s(x(w))$ there exists $n\in N$ with $n\cdot z=w$. If $N$ acts trivially, the equal-value condition demands injectivity of $s\circ x$ on $\mathbb H$. The affine equation has two distinct $Y$-values for some $X$, so surjectivity gives distinct points in $\mathbb H$ with the same $x$-value and rules out this case. Under these hypotheses, $x(n\cdot z)=x(z)$ for every $n\in N$ and $z\in\mathbb H$.

## `canLift27Complex_of_invariant_polynomial_uniformization`

### English

Assume that for every elliptic Weierstrass curve $E$ over $\mathbb C$ with $j(E)\notin\{0,1728,\frac{488095744}{125},\frac{1556068}{81}\}$ there exist $G_1,G_2\in\mathrm{SL}_2(\mathbb R)$, a map $\pi=(x,y)\colon\mathbb C\to\mathbb C^2$, and a polynomial $s\in\mathbb C[X]$ of degree at least one with the following properties. Write $\mathbb H=\{z\in\mathbb C:\operatorname{Im}z>0\}$ and let $\Gamma=\langle G_1,G_2\rangle$ be the subgroup generated by $G_1,G_2$. At each $z\in\mathbb H$ the map $\pi$ is complex differentiable with $\pi'(z)\ne(0,0)$. Its image on $\mathbb H$ is exactly the set of complex solutions $(X,Y)$ of the affine equation $Y^2+a_1XY+a_3Y=X^3+a_2X^2+a_4X+a_6$ with the coefficients $a_i$ of $E$. For every $z,w\in\mathbb H$, the equality $\pi(z)=\pi(w)$ holds if and only if $\gamma\cdot z=w$ for some $\gamma\in\Gamma$, where $\cdot$ is the Möbius action of determinant-one real matrices. Let $N$ be the commensurator of $\Gamma$ inside $\mathrm{SL}_2(\mathbb R)$, the set of $n$ for which $\Gamma\cap n\Gamma n^{-1}$ has finite index in both $\Gamma$ and $n\Gamma n^{-1}$. For every $n\in N$ and $z\in\mathbb H$, $s(x(n\cdot z))=s(x(z))$. For every $z,w\in\mathbb H$ with $s(x(z))=s(x(w))$, there exists $n\in N$ with $n\cdot z=w$. Then for every elliptic Weierstrass curve $E$ over $\mathbb C$ with the same four $j$-invariant exclusions, some affine presentation has a cover to each of $P_0(E)$ and $P_1(E)$. Every affine presentation $Y$ for which some affine presentation has a cover to both $P_0(E)$ and $Y$ has exactly one cover to $P_1(E)$. An affine presentation $T$ consists of a one-dimensional Dedekind domain $R_T$ that is a finitely generated $\mathbb C$-algebra and a natural-number multiplicity function $m_T$ on its ideals. The function is positive at each maximal ideal and equals one at all but finitely many maximal ideals. A cover $Y\to T$ is an injective integral $\mathbb C$-algebra map $f:R_T\hookrightarrow R_Y$ with $e_f(\mathfrak q)m_Y(\mathfrak q)=m_T(f^{-1}(\mathfrak q))$ for every maximal ideal $\mathfrak q$ of $R_Y$, where $e_f(\mathfrak q)$ is the ramification index at $\mathfrak q$ over its contraction. The two normalization presentations use level one and the zero additive subgroup of the elliptic-curve point group. In the ambient field $\Omega(E)$ for the normalization, write $Q_1\in E(\Omega(E))$ for the distinguished level-one point and $x_G$ for the distinguished coordinate generating the base field $\mathbb C(x_G)$. The two fixed fields are $K_0$, fixed by all $\mathbb C(x_G)$-automorphisms of $\Omega(E)$ that fix $Q_1$, and $K_1$, fixed by all $\mathbb C(x_G)$-automorphisms of $\Omega(E)$ that send $Q_1$ to either $Q_1$ or $-Q_1$. For $i=0,1$, the ring $R_{P_i(E)}$ is the integral closure of $\mathbb C[x_G]$ in $K_i$. The multiplicity for $P_0(E)$ is one on every ideal. The multiplicity for $P_1(E)$ is the ramification-index function for the inclusion $R_{P_1(E)}\subseteq R_{P_0(E)}$. At a maximal ideal of $R_{P_1(E)}$ it is the index at the chosen prime of $R_{P_0(E)}$ over that ideal.
